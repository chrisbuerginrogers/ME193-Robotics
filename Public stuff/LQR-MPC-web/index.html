LQR and MPC for a line-following robot

Two ways to steer a two-wheeled robot along a line: a feedback law worked out once on paper (LQR), and a small optimization re-solved at every step (MPC). Everything runs in your browser as Python and numpy through PyScript, so there is nothing to install. You can explore everything in simulation, then connect a LEGO Education robot over Bluetooth on the Robot page.

SimulatorLQR and MPC drive the same track. Move the weights, speed and horizon and see where each one goes blind. LQRThe gains, the damping, the three sensor states, and why a fixed gain fails on a sharp turn. MPCOne solve, candidate by candidate, and how a long horizon turns into LQR. 🔌 RobotConnect a LEGO Education Double Motor and Color Sensor, calibrate, and run bang-bang, PD, LQR or MPC on a real line. NotesThe worked maths: the model, LQR, MPC, and the difference between them.

The first page you open takes about ten seconds to load Python. After that it is quick. The simulator is lqrmpc.py, about 250 lines of numpy.
